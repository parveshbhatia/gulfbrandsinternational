01 — The read

What I found before applying

Six things visible from outside GBI Express. Each one is a lever, not a criticism.

Platform

Legacy storefront

The site runs on an older cart architecture with query-string URLs. That caps page speed, structured data and indexation, and all three feed straight into conversion rate and organic acquisition cost.

Funnel

Age gate as step one

The 21+ check is legally non-negotiable. How it is built is not. It is the first drop-off in the funnel and it should be measured as one, with the exit rate on the weekly report.

Channel mix

Web, iOS, Android

Three surfaces, one basket. Without unified GA4 measurement across app and web, repeat customers get counted as new and acquisition cost per real customer reads worse than it is.

Margin

Standing discounts

20% off selected wines, 20% off selected spirits, 5% off selected beers on registration. A permanent discount is a permanent margin line. It needs a payback model per category, not one blanket rate.

Omnichannel

Click and collect, Adliya

Collection at the retail store is the cheapest fulfilment GBI owns and today it is a footnote. Merchandised properly it lifts contribution margin on exactly the low-value baskets that cost most to deliver.

Retention

Mailing list only

There is a newsletter sign-up and no visible lifecycle programme. Replenishment is the most predictable behaviour in this category. Restock and win-back flows are the fastest revenue available on the existing customer file.

02 — First 90 days

Baseline, then fix, then grow

Sequenced deliberately. Nothing gets optimised before it is measured, and nothing gets scaled before the operation can carry it.

01Days 1–30

Baseline the P&L and the funnel

  • Rebuild the Home Shopping P&L to contribution level per order: revenue, gross margin, delivery cost, payment fees, discount cost.
  • Audit GA4 across web and both apps — event integrity, funnel steps, age-gate exit rate, app versus web attribution.
  • Work a full trading week with the Home Shopping Admin and the drivers, including a late-night shift, and map order to doorstep end to end.
  • Pull 12 months of order data: basket size, category mix, repeat rate, delivery-slot demand, complaints by root cause.
  • Issue the baseline pack. One page, agreed with Finance, signed off by the GM. Every later claim is measured against it.
02Days 31–60

Fix the leaks and lock the SOPs

  • Rewrite checkout and the age-gate flow against the measured drop-off: fewest possible steps to a legal, verified order.
  • Rota built off the demand curve rather than headcount habit — staff and driver capacity mapped to the noon cutoff and the evening peak.
  • SOPs reviewed and reissued for order processing, payment verification, stock allocation, delivery scheduling and complaint resolution, each with a named owner and a service level.
  • Launch lifecycle CRM: welcome, replenishment, win-back and a first-to-second order sequence. This is the same engine that took a fashion business to a 40% repeat rate.
  • Fix the catalogue — accurate titles, sizes, ABV, imagery, availability. Wrong product data is a complaint, a redelivery and a refund at once.
  • Weekly trading meeting with Category, Marketing and Logistics. One agenda, one set of numbers.
03Days 61–90

Grow on proven unit economics

  • Reset the promotional calendar by category payback: bundles, multi-buy and basket thresholds in place of blanket discounting.
  • Push click and collect at Adliya as a deliberate low-cost fulfilment route, with its own target and its own reporting line.
  • Rebuild paid media on contribution margin, not revenue — ROAS, CPA and payback window per campaign, agencies held to a defined floor.
  • Grow AOV through curated cases, mixed packs and occasion-led merchandising across the wine, spirit, beer and RTD portfolio.
  • Table the annual business plan and budget: revenue, gross profit, EBITDA and operational KPI targets, with the platform investment case costed and prioritised.
